## Gameplay

The player first choses who to play against and decides whether or not they want to place a bet of wool.

The game is played by placing your pieces in 2x2 boards, 4 for each player, 8 in total. In hard mode, each player uses a 2×3 board.

Players take turns placing their pieces, with the winner of the previous round going first.

Each player gets a selection of 3 pieces to choose from that changes each time a piece is placed down. After a piece is placed, the opponent places their piece. This continues until both players filled their board.

After this, the Duel Phase begins. If there are pieces with blessings on the board, the effects of the pieces happen depending on their sequence number. In player perspective the sequence goes; 1 (right top), 2 (right bottom), 3 (left top), 4 (left bottom).

There is then a number of 4 duels (6 in hard mode) which are each decided through the Rock-Paper-Scissors principle, with consideration of a piece's blessing. Points (usually one) are awarded for each victorious duel, with the winner of the round being the player with the most points. A player needs to win two rounds to win the game.

If there is a draw after the duel phase, both players win the round. This means that a player can still win the game as long as they have already won the previous round. If there is a total draw, with both players winning two times, the game will end in a tie. As there is no winner in this scenario, bets will not be paid.

## Pieces

There are 36 pieces and 4 categories of pieces; Sword, Shield, Scribe and Shepherd.

Shepherd pieces can only be obtained by completing Aniel's quests against the other Flockade players. Swords, Shields and Scribes each have 10 different pieces while Shepherds have 6. The pieces themselves represent lambs that used to exist and other followers of Yngya.

In general, the following applies:

- Swords win against Scribes and lose to Shields
- Shields win against Swords and lose to Scribes
- Scribes win against Shields and lose to Swords
- Shepherds lose to Swords, Shields and Scribes
- A piece of a certain category ties with a piece of the same category

Swords, Shields and Scribes have "blessed" forms, which have unique effects. The blessings are as follows:

- Just: If duelling against others of its category, this piece wins
- Fallen: When a duel phase begins, all pieces in the same column move down one square
- Risen: When a duel phase begins, all pieces in the same column move up one square
- Prize: If this piece wins a duel, two points are awarded instead of one
- Rotten: When played, blessings on the opposite row are nullified
- Scout: This piece can only be played on an enemy piece to replace it. If there is none, it can be placed as normal
- Mercenary: This piece swaps places with its horizontal neighbor when a duel phase begins
- Marked: When this piece wins a duel, the opponent loses one point
- Trickster: This piece turns into any other piece when a duel phase begins

All Shepherds possess unique blessings, which have unique effects. The blessings are as follows:

- Lone: At the start of their turn, the opponent draws 1 piece instead of 3
- Wandering: Each time this piece moves, gain 2 points
- Guardian: If duelling against a piece marked by the wolf, the whole round is won
- Trickster+: When a duel phase begins, the order of play and blessings is reversed. (Unused)
- Rotten+: When played, all pieces on the opponents side have blessings nullified
- False: If this piece losses a duel, 2 points are awarded
- Golden: All points won in duels are doubled

## Trivia

The name Flockade is a combination of the words Flock, as in a flock or "herd of sheep", and Blockade as "an act or means of sealing off a place to prevent goods or people from entering or leaving."

According to the script files, some piece titles have a context explaining why they are called that.

- Mutton Pieces: It's a reference to the name of sheep flesh.
- Rotten Pieces: "Rotten" is a reference to The Rot from the Woolhaven DLC.
- Fallen Pieces: "Fallen" as in struck down in battle, fallen to the enemy. This could be said to mean that the Aniel of the Fallen Scribe piece is not the same Aniel we see in Woolhaven, since she did not fall in battle.
- Just Pieces: "Just" is in the sense of "Justice", someone who has a strong sense of justice, like a judge.
- Marked Pieces: "Marked" as in "marked by the wolf," scarred or wounded. This might be a reference to Tooth and Claw, an unknown wolf enemy with teeth and claws.
- Mercenary Pieces: "Mercenary" as in someone concerned with making money, or who is hired to help.
- Prize Pieces: "Prize" as in valuable.
- Risen Pieces: "Risen" as in "to rise", or someone who has resurfaced or come back from the depths.
- Scout Pieces: "Scout" as in a lookout, or someone who goes ahead and reports back.
- Trickster Pieces: "Trickster" as in someone who plays a lot of pranks or tricks, like a jester.

Comlambments is a fusion with the word Commandments. It is also a reference to the 10 Commandments from Christian scripture.

Lambkind is a play on the word "mankind" for lambs.
